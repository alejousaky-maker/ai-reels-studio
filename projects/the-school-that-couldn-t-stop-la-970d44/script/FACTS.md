# FACTS — The School That Couldn't Stop Laughing

Status: `verified` = checked against the listed public source; `uncertain` = reported/disputed, worded as such in the script; `fiction` = analogy or invented detail; `approved_by_client` = supplied by the client.

- **verified** — The epidemic began on 30 January 1962 at a mission-run girls' boarding school in Kashasha, Tanganyika, with three girls. ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanganyika_laughter_epidemic))
- **verified** — It affected 95 of the 159 pupils; symptoms included laughter, crying and fainting. ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanganyika_laughter_epidemic))
- **verified** — The teaching staff were unaffected and reported that students could not concentrate. ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanganyika_laughter_epidemic))
- **verified** — The school closed on 18 March 1962. ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanganyika_laughter_epidemic))
- **verified** — In Nshamba, 217 mostly young villagers had laughing attacks over 34 days. ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanganyika_laughter_epidemic))
- **verified** — 14 schools were shut down and about 1,000 people were affected; it died off about 18 months after it started. ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanganyika_laughter_epidemic))
- **verified** — It is explained as mass psychogenic illness; Hempelmann links it to stress from higher expectations after independence. ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanganyika_laughter_epidemic))
- **fiction** — The yawn comparison is a narrative analogy.
