# FACTS — Why Lighthouses Get Dressed Up

Status: `verified` = checked against the listed public source; `uncertain` = reported/disputed, worded as such in the script; `fiction` = analogy or invented detail; `approved_by_client` = supplied by the client.

- **verified** — Lighthouses carry distinctive paint patterns (daymarks) so similar towers can be told apart by day. ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Lookout_Light))
- **verified** — Cape Hatteras Lighthouse has spiral stripes; Cape Lookout Light has a black-and-white diamond (checkered) daymark. ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Lookout_Light))
- **verified** — Cape Lookout Light flashes every 15 seconds (its night light characteristic). ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Lookout_Light))
- **uncertain** — Each lighthouse uses its own flash rhythm at night. ([source](general practice (light characteristic); not every light is unique worldwide))
- **fiction** — The sailor, the keeper and the harbour are fictional; the friend/laugh comparison is an analogy.
