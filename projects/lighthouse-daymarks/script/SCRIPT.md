# Why Lighthouses Get Dressed Up

Language: en · style: storybook · 13 beats · 155 words (~46 s at 3.4 words/s)

**1. [wide, day, curious]** Imagine you're a sailor in the eighteen hundreds, and every lighthouse on the coast looks exactly the same.
   - _see:_ Sunny harbour, boats and the striped lighthouse behind; a sailor walks along the quay.
**2. [close, day, curious]** So which one is home?
**3. [medium, day, whimsical]** By day, a tall white tower looks like every other tall white tower.
**4. [high, day, tense]** Steer toward the wrong one, and your trip could end on the rocks.
**5. [medium, golden, warm]** You see, lighthouses have a secret identity.
   - _see:_ The keeper's cosy room at golden hour.
**6. [two_shot, golden, whimsical]** By day, each one wears its own paint. Stripes, bands, even diamonds.
**7. [low, golden, whimsical]** Sailors call these patterns daymarks.
**8. [wide, golden, curious]** In North Carolina, Cape Hatteras wears a black and white spiral, while nearby Cape Lookout wears giant diamonds.
**9. [wide, night, tense]** But at night, paint is useless, so every lighthouse blinks to its own rhythm.
   - _see:_ Night harbour, lamps glowing, the beacon shining.
**10. [medium, night, curious]** Cape Lookout, for example, flashes every fifteen seconds.
**11. [two_shot, night, warm]** It's like recognising a friend by their outfit in the day, and by their laugh in the dark.
**12. [wide, golden, warm]** So the sailor checks the stripes, counts the flashes, and finally knows the way home.
**13. [close, golden, warm]** All thanks to a lighthouse that got dressed up.
