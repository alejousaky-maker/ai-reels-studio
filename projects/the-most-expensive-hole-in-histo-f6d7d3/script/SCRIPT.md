# The Most Expensive Hole in History

Language: en · style: storybook · 14 beats · 248 words (~73 s at 3.4 words/s)

**1. [medium, day, upbeat]** If you want to build a subway that costs four and a half billion dollars for just three stations, you don't build transit. You build a gold mine.
**2. [wide, day, whimsical]** It's nineteen twenty-nine, and New York promises commuters a brand new subway line under Second Avenue to fix endless overcrowding.
**3. [close, day, tense]** Then the Great Depression hits, World War Two happens, and the project gets quietly buried for nearly a century.
**4. [wide, night, tense]** When they finally restart digging in the two thousands, every single inch of dirt costs more than solid platinum.
**5. [medium, night, curious]** Why? Because underneath the street lies a hundred-year-old maze of mystery pipes, electrical cables, and forgotten steam lines.
**6. [medium, day, tense]** Every time they move a single water pipe, it costs millions. They're literally tunneling through a maze of financial regret.
**7. [wide, day, upbeat]** Fast forward billions of dollars later: they finally open three shiny new stations, about two miles of track.
**8. [medium, day, curious]** That means every single step you walk underground cost roughly a million dollars.
**9. [wide, day, whimsical]** For that money, you could buy every daily rider a brand new car, and still have cash left for a private island.
**10. [medium, night, whimsical]** So why did it cost so much? Consultants, special contracts, and hiring six guys to watch one guy hold a shovel.
**11. [medium, day, upbeat]** And the best part? Phase two is still years from finished, which means the budget is about to enter orbit.
**12. [medium, day, warm]** Congratulations, you've just built the most expensive hole in human history.
